Promote open science through shared data and collaboration

Releases data, code, and documentation and fosters cross-site collaborations to accelerate scientific progress. By making resources public and encouraging standardized practices, they increase transparency, enable secondary analyses, and support large-scale studies that combine datasets to address questions requiring greater statistical power or diverse populations.

Connect tools, standards, and communities

Help existing research initiatives work together through compatible technologies, shared approaches, and coordination between communities.

Groups often develop different tools and ways of organizing research data. We help them learn from one another and make their work easier to use together. That takes conversations and shared decisions as well as changes to software.

Preserve research artifacts

Keep data, code, and other research materials accessible and recoverable over time, including materials hosted on other websites and services.

Research produces more than a published paper. Data, code, notes, and recordings help explain how the work was done. We help researchers keep these materials, find them again, and use them later—even when copies are hosted on other websites and services. This helps people return to their own work and gives others a clearer starting point.

Self-hosted research IT infrastructure

We help researchers run and maintain the services their work depends on. Hosting these services themselves gives groups more control over their data and how people access and use it.

Turn research ideas into working tools

Develop and test software, data standards, and analysis workflows that help researchers put new methods into practice.

New methods become useful when other researchers can run them, inspect them, and build on them. We work with research communities to develop and test software, ways of organizing data, and analysis steps that work together. This helps people use research methods in their own studies.

Understand brain function during everyday experiences

Develop and test better ways to analyse brain activity and behaviour during rich experiences, such as watching a film.

Films combine images, sound, and events that unfold over time. Understanding the brain's responses takes methods that can handle these details together. We develop and test ways to make that analysis more reliable and practical, so researchers can learn more from the same recordings. This builds on Psychoinformatics' objective for naturalistic studies.

Build tools for reproducible, shareable science

The emphasis is on creating software, workflows, and infrastructure that make data processing, analysis, and sharing reliable and repeatable. By providing versioned datasets, pipelines, and tooling (for example for neuroimaging and behavioral measurements), the aim is to reduce technical barriers, minimize errors, and help researchers reproduce results and reuse resources across projects.
